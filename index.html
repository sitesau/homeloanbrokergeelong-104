What Happens in the First Broker Meeting

Expect the first conversation to be about evidence, not rates. A broker will typically want income details, existing commitments, dependants, living costs and your deposit or equity position. They will also want to know the property type and any timing pressure, such as an offer deadline or settlement date, because those details shape which lender policies are realistic.

Before lodging anything, ask how the broker is paid, whether any fee applies, and which lenders were considered. ASIC Moneysmart guidance on home loans supports looking beyond the advertised rate to fees, features, repayment type and whether the loan still suits your circumstances. Complex files, such as self-employed income, investment servicing, equity-funded deposits, bridging needs or a tight settlement, benefit most from this pre-lodgement testing.

Documents and Timing to Settle Early

Pre-approval can sometimes be obtained within a few days when documents are ready and the situation is straightforward, while some lenders may take a week or more. Timing depends on lender workload and whether extra verification is needed, including for self-employed income. That makes document readiness a practical risk control rather than an administrative chore.

  1. Confirm the goal: purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging or review.
  2. List income, dependants, existing commitments and deposit or equity position.
  3. Identify the property type and any timing pressure, such as an offer deadline or settlement.
  4. Ask whether the lender will need extra verification before approval.

A broker can test the likely questions before the application goes in, which reduces the chance of a valuation, document or timing surprise stalling the file later.

Questions That Sharpen the Conversation

Good borrower questions expose trade-offs. Ask how the recommended lender handles your income evidence, what happens if valuation, documents or settlement timing changes, and whether the repayment setup still works if rates or expenses move.

  • First home buyers: ask about the Victorian First Home Owner Grant, First Home Guarantee, LMI, deposit gaps and lender choice.
  • Refinancers: ask whether switching is likely to leave you better off after current rate, fees, offset setup, equity and repayment pressure are reviewed.
  • Investors: ask about interest-only structure, offset use, equity-funded deposit and portfolio servicing.
  • Construction borrowers: ask when funds are released, what evidence the lender needs at each stage, and what happens if timing shifts.
